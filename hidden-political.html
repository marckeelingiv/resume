A Framework
for Principled
Civic Media

Reimagining a moderate Republican group as a media-first organization that earns trust through consistency — and converts that trust into political consequence.

Prepared for internal review Twelve diagrams · one thesis
Author's Note

The instinct is to treat this as a political club with a website. That instinct is wrong. What we are actually building is a media organization with a political consequence — and the difference matters at every level of the operation, from how we hire to how we publish to how we behave when our own side does something indefensible.

The diagrams that follow are not decoration. They are the operating logic of the organization. Each communicates one decision we have either made or must make. Read them in order. Argue with the ones you disagree with. Then we move.

I.

The Core Insight

Thesis Media first.
Politics second.
Trust compounds.

Disaffected Utah Republicans have nowhere credible to go. They are told a bill is conservative when it isn't. They are told a fight is principled when it's posturing. We do not solve this by becoming louder. We solve it by becoming trusted — by applying the same framework to every bill, every politician, every session.

Influence is the byproduct, not the goal. The day we invert that order, we become exactly what we set out to replace.

II.

Organizational Structure

Principle Minimal bureaucracy.
Clear ownership.
Pods, not committees.

"Not too much hierarchy" is a slogan, not a structure. What we want is the opposite of bureaucracy without the absence of accountability. A small Steering Circle sets direction and breaks ties. Pods own their domains. Decisions live as close to the work as possible.

III.

The Content Pipeline

Mechanics Bill in.
Analysis out.
Nothing skips the gate.

The pipeline is the product. A bill enters, gets scored against published principles, gets reviewed for soundness, and — only if it survives — gets published in plain language. Stories that aren't bulletproof are killed. The editor's job is to be willing to kill them.

IV.

Three Content Categories

Sequence Awareness first.
Standards next.
Accountability last.

Not all content carries the same risk. Awareness — votes, hearings, public events — is low-risk and builds the audience. Behavioral standards — calling out attacks on people instead of policies — is the differentiator. Accountability — conflicts of interest, undisclosed relationships — is high-risk and must be earned.

V.

The Scoring Rubric

Foundation Define values.
Publish them.
Apply consistently.

This is the load-bearing piece. "True conservative values" must be defined explicitly, in writing, before a single article is published. Without it, every analysis reads as opinion. With it, our conclusions are defensible because the framework is.

VI.

The Credibility Flywheel

Compounding Slow at first.
Then sudden.
Then irreversible.

Trust does not grow linearly. It compounds. The first six months feel like shouting into a void. Then a few hard calls go right and sources start coming to us instead of the other way around. The flywheel only turns if every step is consistent with the last.

VIII.

Editorial Decision Gate

Discipline Four questions.
Every story.
No exceptions.

IX.

Roles Required

Critical Hire The editor.
Without one,
nothing else matters.

XII.

Mission Hierarchy

Subordination Everything serves
the core product.
Or it gets cut.

The website serves the analysis. The events serve the audience that consumes the analysis. The fundraising serves the editorial team that produces the analysis. If a proposed activity does not trace back to the core product, it does not happen. This is how organizations stay small enough to be excellent.

Unresolved · For Group Discussion

Four questions that
shape everything else

  1. i.
    Who is the editor with spine? This person makes or breaks the operation. The technical infrastructure can be built by anyone. The discipline to kill stories cannot.
  2. ii.
    What is the first bill or issue we cover? Pick one where we can demonstrate the gap between what people were told and what's actually true. The first piece sets the standard for every piece that follows.
  3. iii.
    What do we name ourselves? "Moderate Republican" may signal soft to the very people we want to reach. "Principled" or "Constitutional" framing maps closer to what we actually are — we are not moving to the center, we are holding the original line.
  4. iv.
    Inform-first or outcome-first? Inform-first builds slower but lasts longer and is harder to attack. Outcome-first is more urgent but makes us a target immediately. This decision determines tone, urgency, and how soon we make enemies.
